Lesson 1

Introduction to Project Management

Understanding what projects are, how they differ from operations, and the foundational framework of project management.

What Is a Project?

Key Definition

A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result. Unlike operations (ongoing work that sustains the business), projects end when their objectives are reached or terminated.

Seven Project Attributes

  1. Unique purpose — every project has a distinct goal
  2. Temporary — has a defined start and end
  3. Drives change and enables value creation
  4. Progressive elaboration — details are refined over time
  5. Requires resources — often from various areas
  6. Primary customer or sponsor
  7. Involves uncertainty

Project Constraints

Fig 1 — The Triple Constraint with Quality at the center

Remember This

Quadruple constraint = Scope + Time + Cost + Quality. A project may meet the first three but still fail if quality or sponsor satisfaction isn’t met.

What Is Project Management?

Project management is “the application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements.”

Project Stakeholders

People involved in or affected by project activities:

  • Project sponsor
  • Project team
  • Support staff
  • Customers & users
  • Suppliers
  • Opponents of the project

Project Success Factors

  1. Met scope, time, and cost goals
  2. Satisfied the customer/sponsor
  3. Achieved its main objective (e.g., ROI, making sponsors happy)
Top 3 Success Factors

Adequate funding, staff expertise, and engagement from all stakeholders.

PMI & Certification

The Project Management Institute (PMI) is an international society founded in 1969 with 500,000+ members. PMI provides PMP certification (Project Management Professional).

Ethics in PM

  • Decision-making: Transparency and consideration of other parties
  • Proficiency: Only accept assignments matching your skill set
  • Relationships: Build productive, positive relationships
  • Honesty & integrity: Objectivity and truth

  2. Categorizing IT projects — problem, opportunity, or directive
